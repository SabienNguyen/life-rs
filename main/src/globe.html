A planet, over deep time

Nothing here is drawn. Rigid plates turn about randomly drawn Euler poles; crust floats by isostasy, is destroyed where plates converge and born where they part, and rivers take the rest apart. Volcanoes at those same boundaries feed the air the carbon dioxide that keeps it warm, and rain on warm rock takes it back — which is the loop that keeps a planet habitable while its sun brightens. What grows on it is not drawn either: a biome is a reading of two numbers, so the forests move on their own.
